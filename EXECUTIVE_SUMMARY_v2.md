VAERS COVID Reporting Pattern Analysis

Executive Summary (Version 2.0)

Purpose



This project examined publicly available COVID-19 vaccine adverse event reports submitted to the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) and evaluated whether certain reporting patterns remained after adjusting for available vaccine lot distribution data.



The goal was not to determine causation or estimate true injury rates. Rather, the objective was to identify whether specific manufacturers, vaccine lots, or reporting patterns appeared unusual within the VAERS dataset itself and whether those patterns persisted after accounting for vaccine distribution volume.



Data Sources



The analysis used:



Public VAERS reporting data

CDC/ICAN lot distribution records obtained through public disclosure

Manufacturer information

Vaccine lot identifiers

Serious adverse event indicators

Death reports

Hospitalization indicators

Associated symptom records



The final dataset contained hundreds of thousands of COVID-19 vaccine adverse event reports and millions of distributed vaccine doses represented across identified vaccine lots.



Methodology



The project was conducted in multiple phases.



Phase 1: Baseline Analysis



Initial analyses evaluated:



Reporting volume by manufacturer

Serious adverse event frequencies

Death report frequencies

Symptom clusters

Geographic reporting patterns

Age-adjusted and state-adjusted comparisons



These analyses identified differences in reporting patterns between manufacturers and highlighted several vaccine lots that repeatedly appeared in elevated-reporting analyses.



Phase 2: Denominator Adjustment



A major limitation of lot-level VAERS analysis is that report counts alone provide no information regarding how many doses were distributed for a given lot.



To address this limitation, publicly released CDC lot-distribution records were incorporated.



This allowed calculation of:



Reports per million doses shipped

Serious reports per million doses shipped

Death reports per million doses shipped

Observed versus expected serious-reporting ratios



For each lot, expected serious reports were calculated using the overall Pfizer lot baseline among matched lots with denominator information.



Major Findings

Manufacturer-Level Findings



Several manufacturer-specific reporting patterns remained visible after adjustment for age and state distributions.



The analysis identified consistent differences in reporting frequencies among Pfizer, Moderna, and Janssen products.



These findings are best interpreted as differences in reporting patterns within VAERS and should not be interpreted as direct measures of clinical risk.



Lot-Level Findings



The incorporation of denominator data substantially improved the lot-level analysis.



Several Pfizer lots remained elevated after accounting for doses shipped.



Notable examples included:



Lot	O/E Ratio

GJ3277	5.049

EN6201	2.855

EN6200	2.719

EN6202	2.693

ER2613	2.062



The strongest signal identified was lot GJ3277, which exhibited approximately five times the serious-reporting frequency expected relative to the overall Pfizer lot baseline.



The EN6200, EN6201, and EN6202 lots formed a notable cluster that remained elevated even after denominator adjustment.



Conversely, several lots that appeared unusual when examining raw report counts became much less remarkable after accounting for distribution volume.



This demonstrates the importance of denominator-adjusted analysis when evaluating vaccine lot reporting patterns.



What These Findings Do Not Mean



This analysis does not establish:



Vaccine causation

Manufacturing defects

Product contamination

Formulation differences

Clinical injury rates

Population incidence rates



VAERS is a passive surveillance system and is designed primarily as a signal-detection system.



The presence of a report does not establish that a vaccine caused the reported event.



Similarly, elevated reporting frequencies do not prove that a specific lot was defective or unsafe.



Limitations



Several important limitations remain.



Doses Shipped vs. Doses Administered



The denominator analysis used doses shipped rather than confirmed doses administered.



As a result, reporting rates should not be interpreted as true incidence rates.



Passive Reporting System



VAERS is subject to:



Underreporting

Variable reporting practices

Reporting delays

Duplicate investigations

Stimulated reporting effects

Data Quality



Lot identifiers required normalization and cleaning.



Although extensive validation was performed, public datasets inevitably contain reporting inconsistencies and incomplete information.



Conclusion



The addition of publicly available CDC lot-distribution data materially strengthened the lot-level analysis.



Several Pfizer lots remained elevated after denominator adjustment, including GJ3277 and the EN6200–EN6202 cluster.



These findings warrant further study and independent replication using additional datasets and active-surveillance systems.



The results should be interpreted as denominator-adjusted reporting-pattern findings within VAERS and not as proof of causation or clinical risk.
