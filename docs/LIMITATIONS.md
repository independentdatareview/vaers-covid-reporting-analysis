\# Limitations



\## Important Disclaimer



This project analyzes patterns within VAERS reports.



It does not establish:



\- Causation

\- Clinical diagnosis

\- True incidence rates

\- Vaccine effectiveness

\- Individual patient outcomes



The results should be interpreted as observations of reporting behavior within the VAERS database.



\---



\# VAERS Is A Passive Surveillance System



VAERS is designed as an early-warning safety surveillance system.



Reports may be submitted by:



\- Patients

\- Family members

\- Physicians

\- Nurses

\- Pharmacists

\- Vaccine manufacturers



The presence of a report does not demonstrate that a vaccine caused the reported event.



\---



\# Underreporting



Not all adverse events are reported to VAERS.



The magnitude of underreporting is unknown and may vary by:



\- Event type

\- Time period

\- Public awareness

\- Media attention



\---



\# Stimulated Reporting



Reporting rates may increase following:



\- News coverage

\- Scientific publications

\- Government announcements

\- Litigation activity



This phenomenon may affect observed reporting patterns.



\---



\# No Denominator Data



The most significant limitation of this analysis is the absence of administered-dose data.



This study evaluates:



\- Reports



It does not evaluate:



\- Doses administered



As a result:



Reporting frequency should not be interpreted as incidence.



A manufacturer with more administered doses may generate more reports even if true incidence rates are identical.



\---



\# Reporting Frequency Is Not Incidence



This analysis measures:



\- Report counts

\- Report frequencies

\- Report enrichment



It does not measure:



\- True risk

\- True incidence

\- Absolute event rates



Additional denominator data would be required to estimate incidence.



\---



\# Symptom Matching



Several analyses relied upon symptom-term matching.



Examples:



\- Myocarditis

\- Pericarditis

\- Guillain-Barré Syndrome

\- CVST



Symptom matching may not perfectly capture all clinically relevant cases.



Alternative coding strategies may produce slightly different results.



\---



\# Missing Data



Some VAERS reports contain:



\- Missing age

\- Missing sex

\- Missing state

\- Missing symptom information



These limitations may influence statistical models.



\---



\# Logistic Regression Limitations



Logistic regression adjusts for measured variables.



In this project, adjustments included:



\- Age

\- Sex



Unmeasured factors remain possible.



Examples:



\- Prior health conditions

\- Reporting behavior

\- Vaccination setting

\- Comorbidities



Residual confounding may remain present.



\---



\# Manufacturer Comparisons



Observed differences between manufacturers should not automatically be interpreted as causal differences.



Additional factors may contribute:



\- Population differences

\- Rollout timing

\- Dose schedules

\- Reporting behavior

\- Clinical awareness



Further investigation using active-surveillance systems would be necessary.



\---



\# Findings Not Supported



This project investigated several commonly discussed hypotheses.



The analysis did not identify evidence supporting:



\- Placebo lots

\- Three-tier vaccine formulations

\- Systematic geographic targeting

\- Intentional lot stratification



Failure to identify evidence is not proof that such phenomena cannot exist.



It indicates only that this analysis did not detect reproducible support for those hypotheses within the available data.



\---



\# Recommended Future Research



The most important next step is obtaining denominator data.



Priority datasets include:



\- Doses administered by manufacturer

\- Doses administered by age

\- Doses administered by sex

\- Doses administered by time period



Additional validation using:



\- Vaccine Safety Datalink (VSD)

\- FDA Sentinel

\- Medicare datasets

\- Published epidemiological studies



would strengthen confidence in observed findings.
